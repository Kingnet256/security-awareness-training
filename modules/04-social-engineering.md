# Module 4 — Social Engineering

## The one idea
The easiest system to hack is often a helpful human. Attackers exploit
trust, authority, and routine.

## Common plays
- **Pretexting:** "Hi, IT here — I need your code to fix your mailbox."
- **Authority:** an "executive" urgently demanding a payment or gift cards.
- **Tailgating:** following staff through a secure door.
- **Baiting:** a "lost" USB drive left in the car park.
- **Vishing:** the phishing script above, delivered by phone.

## Your rules of thumb
- **IT will never ask for your password or MFA code.** Nobody legitimate will.
- Verify unusual requests through a **known** channel, not the one they contacted you on.
- It's OK to say *"I'll call you back on the official number."* Real colleagues understand.
- Don't plug in unknown USB devices.

## Practice line
Keep one sentence ready: *"Company policy is that I verify this request
before acting — I'll call you straight back."* It ends 90% of attempts.
