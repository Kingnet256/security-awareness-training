# Module 2 — Phishing & Suspicious Email

## The one idea
Phishing works by creating **urgency** so you act before you think.
Slow down and verify.

## Common signals
- Unexpected request to **log in**, **pay**, or **change bank details**.
- Sender address that's *almost* right (`micros0ft-support.com`).
- Links whose visible text doesn't match the real destination (hover to check).
- Generic greetings, odd grammar, or a tone that pressures you.
- Attachments you didn't expect — especially `.htm`, `.zip`, `.iso`, macros.

## The 4-step check
1. **Pause.** Urgency is the tell.
2. **Inspect the sender** — full address, not the display name.
3. **Don't click.** Navigate to the site yourself, or call a known number.
4. **Report** it (see Module 5). Reporting protects your colleagues too.

## If you already clicked
Don't panic, but act fast: disconnect if prompted to install anything,
**change the password** for that account from a different device, and
**report immediately**. Early reporting massively limits the damage.

## Note on look-alike login pages
Attackers clone real login pages to harvest credentials. The page can look
perfect. Your defenses are: **check the address bar**, **use a password
manager** (it won't autofill on a fake domain), and **MFA**.
