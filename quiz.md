# Knowledge Check (10 questions)

1. Which is strongest? (a) `Summer2026!` (b) `anchor-quartz-willow-nimbus` (c) `P@ss1`
2. True/False: adding `!` to a common word makes it safe.
3. You get an urgent email to "reconfirm your login." First action?
4. What is credential stuffing?
5. Name the three MFA factor *types*.
6. You get an MFA push you didn't request. What does it mean and what do you do?
7. Someone calls claiming to be IT and asks for your MFA code. Response?
8. Best defense against a look-alike login page (name two)?
9. You clicked a phishing link and typed your password. First two steps?
10. True/False: reporting a mistake quickly can get you in trouble.

---

## Answer key
1. **(b)** — length/randomness beats short complexity.
2. **False** — it's a trivial, well-known transformation.
3. **Don't click.** Navigate to the site yourself or call a known number; then report.
4. Reusing username/password pairs leaked from one breach to log into other accounts.
5. Something you **know**, something you **have**, something you **are**.
6. Someone likely has your password. **Deny** it, **change the password**, **report**.
7. Refuse — IT never asks for codes/passwords. Verify via the official number; report.
8. Any two of: check the address bar, use a password manager (won't autofill fakes), use MFA/passkeys.
9. Change that account's password from another device; report immediately.
10. **False** — fast reporting is encouraged and protects everyone.
